About

I design and build organic semiconducting materials — engineering how molecules pack, crystallize, and carry charge, then turning that understanding into working devices.

My doctoral work at UT-Dallas centered on molecular engineering of pyrrole-based semiconductors for thin-film and single-crystal organic field-effect transistors (OFETs). Along the way I've grown single crystals, solved and refined structures by X-ray diffraction, fabricated devices in the cleanroom, and characterized materials across their structural, optical, thermal, and electrical dimensions.

That work spans two worlds. In academia, I chase structure–property relationships — why a molecule assembles the way it does and what that means for performance. Toward industry, the same toolkit maps onto crystallization, polymorphism, purity, and yield: the solid-state problems at the heart of pharmaceutical and materials process development.
